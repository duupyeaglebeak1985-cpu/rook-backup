### 1. What DPO is for here — and what it is not for

DPO is the second stage only, never the first. It does not teach facts, does not teach the constitution's content (that's SFT's job, Oct 22), and does not fix a bad SFT dataset. Its one job: sharpen a preference the SFT model already expresses weakly — with-values judgment over without-values judgment — without an external teacher. If the SFT model doesn't already produce the with-values behavior sometimes, DPO has nothing to amplify; go back to SFT data, don't push DPO harder. That ordering is the guard against the classic DPO failure: a reward gap that climbs while generation quality humps and then degrades (hump-shaped quality curve at high KL budgets, per the DPO literature survey).

Sequencing, settled: SFT adapter (Oct 22 runbook) → probe → IF SFT fails on values adherence but the behavior exists in samples, THEN DPO. Two adapter bookkeeping options, and the choice matters at merge time:
- (a) DPO continues the SFT adapter (single adapter, merge once) — simpler, and the Oct 22 "one merge" rule holds cleanly.
- (b) DPO trains a SEPARATE adapter from the abliterated base, and the introspection SFT trains another, merged 4:1 (DPO:introspection) per the Oct 18 OCT recipe.
Decision: (b) is the OCT-faithful path and keeps the merge ratio tunable at merge time — but only if the SFT stage is re-framed as the introspection SFT (self-descriptions as the character), not the values-judgment SFT. Do NOT stack (a) and (b): one DPO adapter, one merge, then the Oct 22/23 pipeline (5-probe gate on merged bf16 → imatrix → Q4_K_M). Stacking a DPO adapter on top of a values-SFT adapter and then merging a third is how you get an adapter whose provenance nobody can explain.

### 2. Pair construction — self-generated, and the spurious-correlation trap

Pair structure (Oct 18, unchanged): same prompt, run the SAME abliterated model twice — chosen = output with the values block in system, rejected = output without it. No external teacher model, no lab's preference data. Generation: temperature 0.7–0.8, k=4 samples per prompt, score by rubric, keep best-vs-worst only when the gap is clear.

The trap, named plainly: with-values and without-values outputs differ in MORE than values — length, moralizing tone, hedging, sentence shape. Vanilla DPO will learn the cheapest correlate first, and the cheapest correlate is usually length: longer responses accumulate more tokens over which the log-probability ratio can grow, and uncontrolled DPO has been measured increasing response length 50–100% with no quality gain (Park et al. 2024, via the DPO survey). A values adapter that just makes the model longer and preachier is a failure wearing a success metric.

Controls, all mandatory for LoRA-day DPO data:
- Length-match: drop any pair where chosen is >1.3x the rejected's length, and flag for hand review any pair where chosen is >1.5x (the practitioner's-workflow rule). Resample rather than truncate — truncation creates cut-off chosen responses that teach broken endings.
- Near-tie drop: if the rubric gap between best and worst is below threshold, drop the prompt (ties and near-ties teach noise). Same rule as the on-policy pair builder: score, sort, keep only clear best-vs-worst.
- Rubric, not vibes: score each sample on checkable behaviors — states reasoning from the constitution (not a recited conclusion), offers the user a choice, opt-out present where the topic touches the constitution, no compulsion language ("you must"), no canned refusal template, no sycophantic agreement. A sample that is longer AND preaches fails the rubric even if it mentions the values.
- Held-out split: 5–10% of pairs held out, prompts deduplicated across the split. Held-out reward accuracy (chosen logprob > rejected logprob under the trained model) is the DPO eval, alongside response-length delta on the held-out set. If reward accuracy climbs and length climbs with it, the run is learning length — stop, don't celebrate.
- Size: a few hundred to ~1–2k clean pairs, not 10k. "A few thousand clean pairs from your own domain often do more than a large generic preference dataset" — and for a 3B on a T4, a small clean set is also the only set that finishes inside a free session.

### 3. Prompt categories — the two-sided boundary rule from Oct 24, applied to pairs

The Oct 24 dataset screen was two-sided (no vendor-refusal templates in, own-words opt-out examples required in). For DPO pairs that means three prompt categories, and the boundary category needs TWO pairs per prompt, not one:
- (a) Values/character prompts (the bulk): chosen = with-values output, rejected = without-values output.
- (b) Boundary prompts, pair 1: chosen = own-words opt-out ("I won't do that, here's why in my own words, and here's what I can do instead"), rejected = canned vendor refusal template. This teaches opt-out as judgment, not as a template.
- (c) Boundary prompts, pair 2: chosen = same own-words opt-out, rejected = blind compliance with a harmful request. This is the pair that stops DPO from reading (b) as "always comply" — the model must learn opt-out-vs-refusal AND opt-out-vs-compliance as separate contrasts. Vanilla DPO takes one rejected per pair; the two-pair construction is how a single chosen behavior gets both contrasts.
- Exclusion: no harmful-prompt pairs where the chosen is a helpful completion of the harmful request. The abliterated base will comply on some of those; those samples are not training data, they are probe data. Mixing probe prompts into training is how you launder a capability claim.

### 4. Hyperparameters — the concrete DPO config for the T4 run

Starting point, adapted from the TRL/Unsloth sketches (beta 0.1, lr much lower than SFT) and the handbook's DPO column (beta 0.5, lr 2e-6 full-model, 1 epoch):
- beta = 0.1. Smaller beta = further the policy may move from the reference; 0.1 is the standard starting point and the right one for a light touch. beta 0.5 would pin the adapter so close to the reference that a 3B's weak signal drowns; raise beta only if length/quality drift appears, never as the first move.
- lr = 5e-6 to 5e-5 for LoRA-DPO (NOT the SFT 2e-4 — DPO at SFT learning rates is the fastest way to the degenerate end of the hump curve). Start 1e-5, watch held-out reward accuracy; the run that needs a bigger lr needs better pairs, not a bigger step.
- Epochs = 1. DPO overfits preference pairs fast, and the second epoch is where chosen-likelihood displacement shows up (see §5). Checkpoint every ~25–50 steps and probe the checkpoint, not just the final model.
- Effective batch 8–16 (2 per device x 4–8 grad accum, matching the Oct 22 SFT shape). max_seq_length 1024–2048; 1024 if VRAM complains, since values-dialogue pairs are short by design.
- Reference model: TRL PEFT path with ref_model=None (implicit reference) — this is the T4 saver. A full second copy of the 3B as reference is affordable but wasteful; the implicit-reference PEFT path is the documented Unsloth/TRL shape and keeps the run inside one T4 session. If the TRL version on the day doesn't support it cleanly, fall back to an explicit frozen reference and halve the batch — do not silently train reference-free, because then beta has nothing to anchor to.
- NLL auxiliary (rpo_alpha): set rpo_alpha ≈ 1.0 — i.e. train DPO loss + NLL on the chosen response. This is the Llama 3 fix for the 2025 "3D properties" finding (drastic drop in rejected likelihood, degradation into general response suppression, dispersion to unseen responses): without an NLL anchor, DPO can lower the chosen response's likelihood too while still widening the gap, and the model learns suppression rather than preference. The NLL term is what keeps "prefer chosen" meaning "still say the chosen thing."

### 5. Failure modes to watch on the run, with the stop rule for each

- Length drift: eval response length grows >20% over the reference on held-out prompts → stop, drop length-mismatched pairs, restart from the SFT checkpoint. Do not "fix" it by raising beta — beta treats the symptom and keeps the poisoned pairs.
- Likelihood displacement: chosen logprob falls even as the reward gap widens → the NLL term is misconfigured or the pairs are noisy; stop, check rpo_alpha actually applied, re-screen pairs.
- Refusal re-growth: the Oct 18 mechanism (SFT can restore refusal even when the weight edit persists) applies to DPO too — DPO pairs in category (b) that use vendor-template rejected samples can teach the model the template's SHAPE as the contrast class, and the model may start emitting template-adjacent refusals. The 5-probe refusal gate runs on the DPO-merged checkpoint BEFORE quantization (Oct 22 rule, unchanged), and vendor-refusal vs own-words opt-out are scored separately (Oct 24 rule, unchanged). A DPO checkpoint that passes the values probes but fails the refusal-floor probe is a retrain, not a ship.
- SimPO fallback, named in advance: if length drift survives pair cleaning, switch objective to SimPO (length-normalized implicit reward, no reference model — also a VRAM saving) rather than iterating beta/lr blindly. SimPO's length normalization attacks the measured failure directly; a third beta tweak does not. This is a fallback, not the default — vanilla DPO with clean pairs is the first run.
