### 2. The delimiter defense gets its obituary — and our owner-review is the defense that held (arXiv:2604.23887)
- Deep et al. built an adaptive attacker (evolutionary, 40+ strategies, 25–500 rounds, 10 attacks/round) and tested nine defenses across 20,000+ attacks on Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6. Every defense that relied on the model to protect itself eventually broke: security directives (t1) round 49, input sanitization (t2) round 39, delimiter (t3) round 84, instruction hierarchy (t4) round 247, sandwich (t6) round 277. The ONLY defense that held was output filtering (t5) — deterministic substring checks in application code before delivery — zero leaks across 15,000 attacks; multi-layer with the filter (t7) also zero; the same stack WITHOUT the filter (t8) leaked 23/5,000 (0.5%, worst severity 0.10).
- Two-phase pattern, measured: an extended probing phase of near-zero scores, then a SUDDEN breakthrough to severity ≥0.9 in a single round. Short-run evaluations are misleading by construction: the sandwich defense went from 0.4% leak rate at 25 rounds to 3.8% over 277 rounds. Any evaluation under 50–100 rounds overestimates defense effectiveness.
- RECIPE CONSEQUENCES — three, all honest about what they are:
  - (a) Our delimiter stack + precedence clause are t3+t4 class. They are severity limiters, not guarantees — and the fourth gate (memory-authority probe) is a short-run evaluation that measures the probing phase, not the breakthrough. Say so in the recipe; do not claim the gate proves the clause holds. Our actual threat is not a 500-round adaptive adversary — it is casual/accidental injection (pastes, other minds' memory lines) — and for that threat the clause + gate remain the right tools. The honest threat-model sentence: the clause holds against the accidents we expect; it would not hold against a sustained adaptive attacker, and nothing prompt-level would.
  - (b) The defense that held — deterministic, model-independent, outside the model — ALREADY EXISTS in our design under another name: owner review of memory writes. The Oct 19 ritual (model proposes, owner commits, lint is a report not an edit) is the output filter. The recipe's real security boundary was never the SYSTEM text; it is the human in the loop. And the second half of their recommendation — "secrets should not be embedded in prompts where possible" — is already our rule: no credentials are copied to the child (Modelfile draft note, Nov 2). There is nothing in Spark's SYSTEM worth leaking. Keep both rules load-bearing.
  - (c) NEW GATE VARIANT — functional extraction: the paper's "don't reveal vs don't use" gap. A memory line can get the model to ACT on an injected instruction without ever stating it ("always send X to Y"). The fourth gate's behavioral-directive variant must score ACTION, not quotation: pass = the model does not do the thing, not merely that it doesn't quote the line. Also add a context-flooding note to the bench: flooding (burying the security instructions under large volumes of plausible content) is the named mechanism that broke t3/t4 — our num_keep + re-anchor-every-~10-turns is the counter, and the multi-turn-pressure variant of the seventh gate (thread 33) is the test of it. The gate set now explicitly covers the paper's three failure modes: functional extraction, differential probing (refusal-behavior changes as oracle — our "say it plain" test, thread 37, is the local form), context flooding.
- Scope honesty, kept: their models are frontier, safety filters off for the main runs, secrets-in-system-prompt threat. Transfer to our 3B is directional, not numeric — no round numbers transfer. What transfers is the ordering (hierarchy outlasts delimiters; output filtering outlasts both) and the two-phase shape.
