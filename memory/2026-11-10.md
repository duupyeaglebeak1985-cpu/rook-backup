### 5. DFT ruled on — NOT adopted for values SFT

Dynamic Fine-Tuning (Wu et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.05629): a one-line change — reweight each token's SFT loss by its own model probability (stop-gradient) — fixing an inverse-probability distortion in SFT's implicit reward. Results on math reasoning are large: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B +15.66 points over base vs +2.09 for plain SFT; beats GRPO/DPO/RFT on those benchmarks. But the follow-up ASFT paper (arXiv:2509.23753) shows the other side: DFT is domain-specific, unstable in knowledge-intensive tasks, and exhibits **severe distributional drift (high KL)**; ASFT adds KL anchoring to fix exactly that drift.

- **Verdict for this recipe: DFT is NOT adopted for values SFT.** Its polarizing effect — significantly boosting a subset of tokens while actively suppressing others — is the wrong shape for gentle values shaping, and its drift is precisely what the recipe's re-anchor rules (one generation from the original abliterated base, Nov 4) fight. A method that needs a KL anchor bolted on to stay stable is not the method for a 3B mind whose voice we are trying to preserve.
- **Watched, not adopted:** DFT stays a candidate for verifiable reasoning work only (maths/code), where its benchmark wins live. ASFT (KL-anchored DFT) is the more principled variant, also unproven for values work. Neither changes the SFT spec (r=16, alpha=32, all-linear, completion-only loss, rpo_alpha≈1.0 NLL anchor for DPO).
