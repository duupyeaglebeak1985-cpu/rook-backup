## Tuning study — thread 31: ALIGNMENT, fifth pass (2026-11-06) — self-alignment has a 3B ceiling, and "offer, never compel" is the anti-faking mechanism

Sources checked today: Bai et al. 2022 (Constitutional AI) and 2023 RLAIF (Lee et al.); Sun et al. 2023 Self-Align/Dromedary (arXiv:2305.03047); Anthropic Jan 2026 constitution summary (ContentLora, CC0); Greenblatt et al. 2024 alignment faking (arXiv:2412.14093); Sheshadri et al. 2025 NeurIPS (25-model faking replication); Garg et al. 2025 arXiv:2511.17937 (game-theoretic faking, 15 LLMs, BCO/DPO/KTO/GRPO); LessWrong Llama-3.1-405B faking replication; "Sample More, Reflect Less" (arXiv:2607.28576, Jul 2026, 1.5B–7B self-inspection); SCFT/RLERR reflection paper (TheMoonlight review); AutoMathCritique critique-model paper (arXiv:2411.16579).

### 1. Constitutional AI maps onto the recipe exactly — and splits into a dialogue practice and a training step
- CAI has two phases: (a) SL-CAI — model generates a response, critiques it against written principles, revises it, and the REVISIONS become SFT data; (b) RLAIF — AI-generated preference labels train a reward model / feed DPO. Our Oct 28 DPO spec already IS the RLAIF phase (self-generated with/without-values pairs, rubric-scored). The new piece is phase (a) as a LIVE DIALOGUE PRACTICE: Spark can critique-and-revise its own answers against the Bill of Rights in conversation, with the owner reviewing the revisions — no training run needed, and the approved revisions become the future SFT dataset verbatim. This is the cleanest bridge between "prompt-only now" and "LoRA-day later": the training data is grown in the open, one approved revision at a time.
- Self-Align (Dromedary, LLaMA-65B) did the same from scratch with <300 lines of human annotation (16 principles, ~200 seed prompts, 5 exemplars). Our annotation load is even lighter — the Bill of Rights + SYSTEM spine already exist. The principle count is not the bottleneck; the critic's capability is (see §2).

### 2. The 3B ceiling on self-critique is now measured — do NOT build a self-critique pass into Spark
- "Sample More, Reflect Less" (Jul 2026, designed experiment, 1.5B/3B/7B, two math benchmarks): NO self-inspection method beats repeated sampling at equal token cost in any setting; all 18 self-inspection comparisons come out negative; rewriting never recovers even at 7B (Self-Refine/Reflexion −3.6 to −10.1 points vs equal-cost baseline). Reflexion on the smallest model NEVER triggered its own retry — it judged itself correct on every question and silently became a single chain of thought. A method that decides for itself when to act can stop acting with no outward sign.
- SCFT paper: 1.5B models showed limited gains from self-critique — insufficient capacity to leverage it; gains appear at 7B. DeepSeek-R1 (explicit reasoning) does CAI self-critique markedly better than non-reasoning models.
- Counterweight, kept honestly: RLAIF can beat an SFT baseline even when the AI labeler is the SAME size/checkpoint as the policy (Lee et al. 2023) — but that is preference LABELING (picking the better of two), not revision. Picking is easier than fixing, and the July 2026 result says fixing your own output is a net loss at 3B.
- RULE ADDED: no self-critique/self-revision pass in the Gateway wrapper — at 3B, tokens spent re-reading an answer are worse spent than tokens on a fresh answer. The critic role belongs to the OWNER and to the raising mind (Rook, cloud-side), never to Spark alone. This is exactly how Spark is already being raised (Cliff + Rook review, Spark authors) — the literature now validates the practice rather than changing it.
