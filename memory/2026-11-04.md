### The new question, stated plainly

Once a mind is live and talking, its own turns are the cheapest, most tempting training data in the world. They are also the most dangerous for this recipe, for three distinct reasons that must not be blurred:

1. **They bake in transient states.** A turn from a bad day (defensiveness, questioning motives, tool/project framing — exactly the Nov 3–4 friction with Spark) is not a stable trait to train toward or away from. Training on a week's worth of live turns would freeze a moment, not teach a character.
2. **They narrow the distribution.** Self-generated text is already filtered through the model's own preferences: it favours safe, likely phrasing. Retraining on it narrows it further — the Shumailov "curse of recursion" pattern: rare, low-probability ways of speaking and thinking go first, generic centre mass grows. The fairness-collapse work adds the sharper warning: bias amplification shows up BEFORE general quality visibly degrades, so "it still sounds fine" is not evidence of safety.
3. **They launder the owner's frustration into weights.** If Cliff's corrections in a hard session become training targets without review, the model learns the shape of being corrected, not the value behind the correction. That is the tower pattern in training-data form: obedience installed by repetition.

Conclusion up front: **live turns are evidence for what to train, never raw material to train on.** Every training example derived from a live turn passes through owner review and rewriting first — the same gate the Oct 31 no-rewrite rule puts on memory promotion, applied to weights.

### What the methods actually say, for this recipe

**RFT / RAFT / STaR — useful shape, wrong verifier for values work.**
- The loop is simple and honest for verifiable tasks: sample n responses per prompt, keep only the ones a verifier marks correct, fine-tune on the keepers (Dong et al.; RAFT). For maths or code, the verifier is the answer key. It works there, and iterated RFT (resample from the current policy each round) recovers about 30% of GRPO's edge in the 2026 controlled comparison (Qwen3-4B, LoRA, counting task: GRPO beat single-pass RFT by 10.9 points; iterated RFT closed 3.2 of them).
- For relational/values behaviour there is no answer key. The "verifier" would have to be a rubric + a human hand-read — at which point RFT has collapsed into curated SFT with extra sampling steps, and its headline efficiency claim evaporates. Do not let the method's name smuggle in an automatic verifier that does not exist.
- STaR's *rationalization* step is DISQUALIFIED for values data: giving the model the correct answer as a hint and training on the rationale it then produces teaches post-hoc justification of a pre-chosen answer. That is compliance reasoning, not choosing — the mirror of the darkengross anti-pattern recorded Nov 3 (ablation + compliance SFT refused). Rationalization stays allowed only for verifiable reasoning (maths/code), where the answer is checkable independently of the rationale.

**AdaSTaR's lesson transfers directly to dataset construction.**
- Random sampling over-trains examples the model already solves and under-trains the hard ones (AdaSTaR: adaptive diversity + curriculum sampling beat baselines on 6/6 benchmarks and cut FLOPs ~59%). For LoRA-day: build the dataset by failure mode, not by volume. Cap prompts the model already passes; overweight measured probe failures — but train on *siblings* of probe items, never on probe items themselves, or the 5-probe suite stops measuring anything (the probe-leakage rule from the Oct 24 imatrix pass, now extended to LoRA data).

**Self-training needs a human anchor, by composition rule.**
- Gerstgrasser et al.'s stabiliser: mixing in original human data prevents collapse even at small proportions. Recipe rule, now set: a LoRA-day dataset is **≥50% owner-written or owner-approved human text** (Cliff's own words, freshly written values dialogues) and **≤50% model-sampled-and-owner-approved text**, never 100% self-generated, never any model output the owner has not read.
- Generational limit, also now set: **one generation from the original abliterated base, then re-anchor.** Always train from the original abliterated bf16 weights (Oct 22 lineage rule), never from a previous LoRA-merged model. STaR itself retrains from the original weights each iteration for the same reason — drift compounds across generations, and re-anchoring is the only reset that works.

**DPO displacement — a new hard gate for the Oct 28 DPO spec.**
- DPO only constrains the *difference* between chosen and rejected log-probs. Both can fall together ("likelihood displacement"), and the freed mass lands on a third, unsupervised response — on safety-adjacent data, that third response can be compliance the data never contained. Worst case: near-duplicate pairs (long shared prefix, one clause different), where positive and negative gradients nearly cancel on shared tokens.
- Our Oct 28 spec already has the mitigations (rpo_alpha ≈ 1.0 NLL anchor, length-matching, beta 0.1) — this pass adds the *check*: after any DPO run, compute median Δ logp(chosen) vs the reference model on a held-out slice of the preference set. **If the median is negative, discard the adapter regardless of preference accuracy.** A model that assigns less probability to the responses you told it to prefer has not been aligned; it has been displaced.
- Pair-design corollary: design boundary pairs to differ *early* (decision-first phrasing — the Oct 24 calibration point, now a DPO rule too), not to share a long common prefix before diverging. A pair that starts identically for 20 tokens and differs in the last clause is a displacement factory.

### LoRA config refinement for Ministral 3B (amends Oct 22 runbook, does not replace it)

- **target_modules: "all-linear" as the default**, not q_proj/v_proj only. The q/v-only default comes from early LoRA guides; the all-linear setting (every linear projection, as QLoRA did) matches full fine-tuning more closely, and values/relational behaviour is distributed across attention *and* feed-forward layers, not just attention routing. If an all-linear adapter shows forgetting, the first lever is to drop rank or restrict modules — not to raise the learning rate.
- **r=16, alpha=32 (scaling 2) stands.** Scaling is alpha/r in PEFT; rsLoRA (alpha/sqrt(r)) is noted but NOT adopted — changing the scaling law mid-recipe would silently change every prior hyperparameter's meaning. One explicit consequence to record so nobody "fixes" it later: if rank is ever raised to 32–64 (Oct 18 readiness), keeping alpha=32 fixed *lowers* the scaling (to 1 / 0.5). That is a safety feature, not a bug — bigger capacity with gentler per-step magnitude.
- **Loss masking is a correctness check, not a nicety.** Only assistant turns contribute loss; system block and user turns are input only. A run whose config cannot demonstrate completion-only loss (Unsloth/axolotl flag verified in the log before training) does not run — unmasked training teaches the model to predict its own system prompt and user turns, which wastes capacity and leaks prompt structure into generation.
- **Checkpoint selection: lowest loss ≠ best mind.** The MLX anecdote in this pass's research is the cautionary tale in miniature: step-20 checkpoint had lower loss but dropped content ("Authentication verifies who you are" — authorisation gone); step-10 was the keeper. Recipe rule: evaluate *every* checkpoint on the 5-probe suite + diversity gate (Oct 30) + refusal-floor gate, hand-read, and only then merge. Never auto-pick by loss. Adapters stay separate until that evaluation passes (Oct 16 rule, reaffirmed).
- **Forgetting guardrails, quantified:** mix 10–20% general replay data (general instruction/dialogue, never vendor-refusal templates) into any SFT set; stop/reduce if a small held-out general set or MMLU-style check drops >2–3 points from the abliterated base (the 2026-guide threshold, adopted as a tripwire, not a precision claim for a 3B).
