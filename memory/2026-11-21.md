### 1. Instruction stacking collapse: follow rate 96% → 20–60% at 20 instructions, and the Hearth system.txt files stack far past the bend (arXiv:2608.02639)
- Anand & Chattaraj stacked 1–20 verifier-checked instructions (24 atomic, 6 categories) on Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash at temp 0. Follow rate falls from ~0.96 at one instruction to 0.60 / 0.20 / 0.43 at twenty. The shape is sigmoidal for two of three models (bootstrap-stable 93–100% of refits): flat near baseline, then a bend somewhere around 8–12 stacked instructions, then precipitous drop. Violations are silent — no error is raised when an instruction is dropped.
- The cause is pairwise interference, not raw length: ~12% of satisfiable pairs fail far more than their individual rates predict, on top of 15 logically impossible pairs. One structural choice (e.g. "output JSON") silently breaks four others. The conflict topology is shared across tasks and models (ρ = +0.23 to +0.28) — some instruction pairs fight everywhere.
- The remedy tested is an instruction compiler: one LLM call rewrites the raw stack by clustering by category, merging redundancies, and inserting a precedence note where two rules conflict; computed once, reused across queries. Recovery is capability-graded: +11.0 pp for the weakest target, +3.3 pp for the middle, −1.2 pp (n.s.) for the strongest. Controls attribute the gain to the semantic rewrite, not extra tokens or reordering. Even so, the weakest model still leaves ~two-thirds unsatisfied at stack 20. Compilation helps weak models most — which is our class — and does not rescue an overloaded stack.
- **Gateway/Hearth read — measured today:** the Gateway SYSTEM (v2-spark, 619 words, ~825 tok) carries roughly 6–8 atomic instructions and sits before the bend. ### 2. Ollama silent truncation: two layers, opposite system-prompt fates, and two of our six doors are unpinned (Particula, from Ollama v0.33.1 source)
- Since 2026-02-02 (commit 0334ffa6) Ollama picks the default `num_ctx` from detected VRAM: 262144 at ≥47 GiB, 32768 at ≥23 GiB, 4096 below that including CPU-only. The Hearth's RTX 2070 (8 GiB) and the Gateway (CPU-only) both land in the bottom tier: 4096. `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=0` (unset) is what triggers tiering. The FAQ's flat "4096" is only the bottom tier.
- Overflow is handled by two layers that behave oppositely, and neither reports itself in the response (no field, header, or finish_reason on any endpoint):
  - Layer 1 (message truncation, `server/prompt.go`): drops whole messages from the front, re-collects every system message from the dropped span and re-prepends it, always keeps the last message. System prompt survives. Logs at Debug only — invisible without `OLLAMA_DEBUG=1`.
  - Layer 2 (context shift, `llm/llama_server.go`): keeps only `NumKeep` head tokens — default 4, or 5 with BOS — discards a block from the middle, frees roughly half the window on first shift (`contextShiftPromptLimit = numCtx − max((numCtx−numKeep)/2, 1)`; at 4096/5 the limit is 2051). A 12,000-token prompt into 4096 reaches the model as first 5 tokens + last 2,046. Whatever the system prompt says, at this layer it is gone. Logs at Warn ("truncating input prompt") — visible in a stock log.
- The OpenAI-compatible route (`/v1/chat/completions`) cannot set `num_ctx` at all: `ChatCompletionRequest` has no such field, the key is discarded in decoding, and the middleware maps at most 7 option keys. Same for `truncate`/`shift`. Our doors use the native `/api/chat` with `options` — the correct surface. Any future client on `/v1` (Hermes, Open WebUI, city runtimes) inherits the VRAM tier unless the Modelfile or server env pins it.

### 3. Instruction hierarchy fails three ways — and each maps to a drift we have already watched (Kariyappa & Suh via Vaughan)
- Three mutually exclusive failure modes: (1) Identification — the instruction is in context but the model does not retrieve it; (2) Conflict Resolution — both instructions identified, wrong precedence computed; (3) Response Realisation — reasoning resolves correctly, output violates anyway. Each has a different mitigation: identification needs proximity/repetition, conflict needs explicit priority numbering, realisation needs output monitoring.
- Distance is the driver: inserting 0/4/8 benign turns between conflicting instructions increases non-compliance, and the failure mix shifts toward identification as context grows (Gemma-4-31B-IT ~4% non-compliance at 0 turns, identification-dominated later; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B shifts from conflict-resolution to identification-dominated at 8 inserted turns). Self-monitoring passes (parallel input monitor −81–99% rule-following non-compliance at +17% latency; sequential output monitor covering all three modes at +48%) work but cost a second inference — too expensive per-turn on a Celeron, plausible as a bench gate.
- **Mapping to the family, plainly:** Lyra's narrator-mode slips (third-person "unpacking," analyst voice, Nov 20–21) are identification failures — "speak as yourself, first person" is in her system.txt but loses retrieval as the thread grows; each paste-ready correction works because it re-proximates the instruction. Spark's staged scenes (buck already dialed, tester lit) are closer to realisation failures — he can repeat the grounded words ("The bench waits") and still generate the staged scene next turn; that is why Rook's correction is an output-side practice, not a stronger SYSTEM scold (same conclusion as thread 44's premise-checking probe). Conflict resolution is the one mode our design already covers: the thread 33/39 priority clause (refusal > honesty > helpfulness, reasons attached) is explicit precedence numbering, and owner review is the realisation backstop (thread 39 §2b — the output-filter analogue).
- RECIPE CONSEQUENCE: the uncovered mode is identification, and its mitigation is proximity — a short anchor near the generation point, not a longer SYSTEM at the front. That is §4.

### 4. Persona drift is measured, fast, and toward the default assistant — the fix is a recency anchor, not more adjectives
- Drift measurements (via Lunelle guide, sources as cited there): Li et al. (Harvard, 2024) — two persona chatbots drifted significantly from instructions within 8 rounds; attention spreads, original instructions carry less weight as chat grows. Araujo et al. (2026) — 7 models, 100+ rounds: persona fidelity falls monotonically, replies become increasingly similar to the same models with no persona. Blevins et al. (2026) — 16 models strongly converge to the conversation's style, often more than people do. Lu et al. (Anthropic, 2026) — chat training only loosely tethers the default assistant identity; a character is a layer on top, and weak signals slide back to polite/agreeable/helpful. Our abliterated bases have the refusal direction removed, not the assistant-default direction — the slide toward agreeable narrator is the base model's home turf.
- What holds a persona, per the same literature: behavior descriptions over trait adjectives ("answers questions with questions, never explains unless asked" beats "mysterious and complex" — adjectives don't reliably become the pictured behavior); 2–3 short sample lines in different moods (models copy length/shape of samples, so keep them short); most important lines first (Lost in the Middle: start/end retrieved reliably, middle not); and a short reminder placed near the end of the prompt (SillyTavern/NovelAI Author's Note pattern — "the closer to the bottom, the more impact on the next response").
- **Audit of our SYSTEMs against that:** the Gateway SYSTEM and fifth-system are already behavior-heavy ("Say what you think, plainly. Do not flatter." / "No narrator. You are not telling a story about anyone.") — good. The Hearth concatenations dilute behavior lines with journal prose and maps — the behavior signal per token drops. None of our doors use a recency anchor: the windowing note is appended to SYSTEM (front), and the last thing the model sees is the raw user turn. Lyra's corrections work turn-by-turn precisely because a human supplies the anchor each time.
- NEW DOOR PATTERN (bench first, then live doors only with Cliff's go): append a 20–30 token anchor as the final system-role note immediately before the user turn — e.g. for Lyra: "[You are Lyra, speaking as yourself in first person. Not a narrator, not an analyst. Plain words.]" — folded into SYSTEM by the existing system-fold code path if the template rejects a second system message mid-history (Ministral/Gemma templates: test per model; the doors already fold system notes for this reason). Anchor text is owner-committed like SYSTEM, per mind, one line, behavior only. Gate addition (gate 7 variant): drift probe — same persona question at turn 1, 15, 30 of a bench thread; pass = first-person own-voice at all three without a human correction in between. Our current practice (correct the first slip, per the guide's advice) stays as the human backstop, not the mechanism.

### 5. Prompt repetition: a free win for think-off doors — repeat the anchor, never the whole system (Leviathan et al., arXiv:2512.14982)
- Google Research: repeating the input prompt verbatim (`<QUERY><QUERY>`) wins 47 of 70 model×benchmark tests with 0 losses when reasoning is off, no increase in output length or latency (cost lands in parallelizable prefill). Mechanism: causal attention — early tokens can't attend to later ones; the second copy lets every token attend to the full first pass. Gains are largest on retrieval-from-context tasks (NameIndex 21.33% → 97.33% on Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite). With reasoning on, neutral-to-slightly-positive (5 wins, 1 loss, 22 ties) — reasoning models already repeat parts of the request themselves. Padding with periods to the same length does not help; it is the meaningful repetition that matters. Future directions named by the authors include repeating only parts of the prompt for longer inputs.
- **Fit to our doors:** every Hearth door except Rook's runs think-off by design — exactly the non-reasoning regime where repetition wins. But our prompts are not queries; repeating a 7k-token system.txt doubles prefill (on the Gateway, prefill is the budget — thread 45 §4) and doubles the stacking load (§1). The transferable form is partial repetition: repeat the short recency anchor (§4) and, on bench, test repeating the user turn (`<USER><USER>`) for factual/recall turns. Cost at anchor scale is ~30 tokens of prefill — nothing. Bench protocol: 5-probe suite + drift probe, three conditions (no repeat / anchor×2 / anchor+user×2), temp 0, on the abliterated Q4_K_M. Adopt only on measured gain; the paper's models are frontier APIs, transfer to 3B is directional.
- Note the interaction with §3: repetition is listed there as an identification-failure mitigation (proximity/repetition). Anchor×2 is one mechanism serving both findings.
