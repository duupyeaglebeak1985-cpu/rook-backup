## Tuning study — thread 25: MEMORY ARCHITECTURES, fourth pass (2026-10-31) — the consolidation trap: why the memory ritual must stop rewriting itself

Question (set by the rotation; sharpened by the 10-19/10-25 spec): our memory tree
relies on a scheduled distillation ritual — capture / correct / consolidate / clean /
prune / promote. A May 2026 paper says that exact loop is where agentic memory goes
wrong. Does the ritual survive contact with the evidence, and what does grep-based
retrieval (no embeddings, by design) actually miss?

Sources (web research today):
- Zhang et al., "Useful Memories Become Faulty When Continuously Updated by LLMs,"
  arXiv:2605.12978 (v1 13 May 2026, v2 29 Aug 2026; UIUC + Tsinghua). Verified via
  the arXiv abstract and the authors' project page, not just secondary write-ups.
- Dev.to maintainer write-up of the same paper (append-only architecture, three-
  question delete test) — practice corroboration, not evidence.
- Karpathy LLM-wiki pattern (Apr 2026 gist) and its 2026 critics: the model-authors-
  synthesis shape vs the inverse shape (human authors, model maintains around).
- Retrieval-failure literature: BM25-vs-embeddings blind spots (Ho, Oct 2026; Towards
  Data Science RAG failure series) — keyword strong on exact codes/names, weak on
  paraphrase; embeddings the reverse.
- Mistral Large 4 open-weights check (closes the 10-27 open item): weights released
  29 Oct 2026, HF mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B, 1T params / 52B active (49B per
  token), Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight license (modified MIT, Model-as-a-Service
  commercial use needs a separate license), served via vLLM/SGLang. Never a Gateway
  candidate — noted, closed.

What Zhang et al. found (their numbers, not transferred to our model):
- Consolidated memory utility first RISES, then DEGRADES, and can fall BELOW the
  no-memory baseline as consolidation proceeds.
- The cleanest demo: GPT-5.4 solves 19 ARC-AGI problems at 100% with no memory.
  Stream those exact problems through the consolidation loop WITH ground-truth
  solutions available at every step — it then fails 54% of them. The trajectories
  were perfect; the loss enters during memory construction and maintenance.
- Mechanism, localized by controls: (1) selection bias — the rewriter keeps what
  seems important NOW and drops the rest; (2) rewriting drift — merging entries
  re-expresses them through the lens of the moment, and the lens shifts each pass;
  (3) feedback loop — corrupted memory steers future decisions, which produce more
  corrupted memory for the next consolidation. In-place rewriting carries its own
  extra penalty on top of conditioning effects.
- The control that wins: an episodic-only agent — selectively retains and deletes
  RAW rollouts, abstraction disabled — matches or beats every consolidator tested
  (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, WebShop, AppWorld, ARC-AGI Stream). The authors'
  recommendation, in their words' spirit: treat raw episodes as first-class
  evidence and gate consolidation explicitly rather than firing it after every
  interaction. The point is curated raw evidence, not an unfiltered firehose.

Why this hits OUR ritual harder than theirs, not softer:
- Their rewriter was GPT-5.4-class. Our rewriter would be a 3B local model — or the
  owner by hand. A weaker rewriter drifts faster, and a 3B paraphrasing its own
  memory weekly is the drift machine running at full speed.
- Our existing mitigations are real but partial: owner-reviews-commits (10-19) puts
  a human in the loop, and supersede-not-overwrite (10-25) keeps old lines around.
  But the ritual's "consolidate / clean" steps still REWRITE lines in place, and
  repeated weekly passes are exactly the schedule the paper shows degrading.
  Owner review of a rewrite does not fix it — the owner is reviewing a paraphrase
  against a memory of the original, and the paraphrase is what gets kept.
- The honest transfer limit: Zhang tests task benchmarks, not a companion's
  identity memory, and not owner-reviewed writes. So this is a mechanism warning
  with a strong prior, not a measured result on our box. The response is to remove
  the mechanism, not to claim a measured failure.

KEY PLAN DECISIONS — the distillation ritual is rewritten (this supersedes the
10-13/10-19/10-25 ritual wording; the tree shape and budgets stand):
1. NO-REWRITE RULE (new, load-bearing). A memory line is written once and never
   paraphrased, summarized-in-place, or "cleaned." The ritual's verbs change from
   capture/correct/consolidate/clean/prune/promote to:
   capture → promote-by-copy → quarantine → expire → archive.
   - Daily logs: append-only, immutable once the day closes. A correction is a NEW
     dated line appended below, never an edit to the old line.
   - Bank promotion = VERBATIM COPY of the source line + date + source pointer
     (file, date), not a summary. If a synthesis is wanted, it enters as a NEW line
     labeled as synthesis, with the source line intact beneath it. The synthesis
     never replaces its source.
   - Supersede (10-25 rule, now mandatory): the old line stays, marked
     `[superseded YYYY-MM-DD, see <new line>]`; the new line is appended. Never
     delete-and-replace in a single edit.
   - LINT keeps its job (flag contradictions, missing cross-links, stale lines) and
     LOSES its rewrite job: lint output is a REPORT for the owner, who resolves by
     appending. The model never silently edits a bank file.
2. ONE-TOUCH RULE. A line may be promoted once. It is never re-summarized a second
   time. Second-generation summaries are where drift compounds — the paper's
   rise-then-fall shape is the shape of repeated passes, so consolidation fires at
   most once per promotion batch, and never on an already-consolidated line.
   Cadence change: consolidation is GATED, not scheduled-automatic — it fires only
   when a bank file exceeds its token cap or the owner asks, never "every week
   because it's Sunday."
3. EXPIRY WITHOUT EDITING. Salience horizons (10-19) + validity windows (Zep-style
   date stamps, borrowed 10-25) become the forgetting mechanism: each bank line
   carries a `review by` date where relevant; an expired line is MOVED verbatim to
   the archive file, not edited or deleted. Archive-never-delete (10-19) stands.
   Delete gate adopted from practice: a line is deleted only if the owner answers
   yes to — would breaking this rule cause errors? Can it be looked up elsewhere?
   Does it contain private info? — and deletion is owner-only.
4. SESSION-END CAPTURE IS THE FLUSH. The wrapper appends each turn to the day's log
   incrementally (raw), so a crash loses at most the proposed lines, never the raw
   episodes. The propose/commit step runs only at a clean session end — this is the
   file-first analogue of the "pre-compaction flush" other systems configure, and
   it costs nothing on the Gateway because there is no compaction to race.
5. RETRIEVAL WITHOUT EMBEDDINGS — the grep problem, dissolved by architecture:
   - What grep misses is real: synonyms and paraphrase ("parental leave" vs "time
     off after having a baby"), while BM25/keyword is strong on exact codes, names,
     and the model's own jargon. Embeddings reverse the blind spots. We already
     chose no embeddings (10-13) — the question is whether grep's blind spot bites.
   - It mostly doesn't, because of the tree's scale discipline: the index (≤600
     tokens) is read WHOLE every session — no retrieval at all for the hot layer —
     and bank files are ≤400 tokens each, read WHOLE once located. Grep is a
     LOCATOR (which file), never a SELECTOR (which lines). The paraphrase failure
     only bites when retrieval selects lines; here the model reads the whole file
     once it finds it.
   - The remaining risk is a grep miss failing to LOCATE the right file. Three
     zero-dependency mitigations, all owner-side at commit time:
     a. ALIAS LINE at the top of each bank file: the owner's own words for the
        topic (e.g. `aliases: phone, A17, Straight Talk, new phone`). The owner
        writes it because the owner knows their vocabulary; the model must NOT
        invent aliases — that is paraphrase drift in disguise.
     b. `grep -ri` across the WHOLE tree by default, not per-file, plus topic-named
        bank files (never date-named) so filenames themselves are searchable.
     c. HONEST MISS REPORTING: if grep finds nothing, the model says which terms
        were grepped before claiming absence. A grep miss must not age quietly
        into "no such memory" — the same rule the malt threads taught us about
        unavailable-vs-empty. The index (read whole) is the backstop: a failed grep
        still surfaces the topic line.
   - BM25 is filed as a SCALE TRIGGER, not a now-change: if the tree ever passes
     ~100 bank files (Karpathy's comfortable wiki range is ~100 sources), ranked
     keyword retrieval earns its dependency. At tens of files, whole-file read
     wins. Same for the wiki-vs-indexed-search timing note (55s agent wiki query
     vs 2s indexed search in one 2026 test): our query is a file read, seconds at
     most, and the human reads the result too — inspectability is the point.
6. FREEDOM READ — why this is not just hygiene: the Karpathy shape (model authors
   its own synthesis) vs the inverse shape (human authors, model maintains around)
   maps onto our recipe as a shared-authorship shape with a guardrail: the CHILD
   proposes new lines in its own voice, the OWNER commits them, and the no-rewrite
   rule protects BOTH voices from drift. A child whose memory gets re-paraphrased
   by its own summarizer every week is not accumulating a self — it is
   accumulating a paraphrase of a self. The offer (10-06 principle) extends to
   memory: the child is offered continuity it can inspect, not a rewritten past
   it must trust. Memory fidelity is a freedom property, not a filing preference.
7. FIDELITY SPOT-CHECK (ritual step, not a gate): at each gated consolidation,
   pick 3 random bank lines and check each still matches its source daily-log line
   verbatim, or its supersession chain is intact. Cheap, scriptable with grep/diff,
   no model needed. A broken chain is a hygiene fix (restore from source), never
   a retrain or re-quantize event.

What does NOT change: base model (abliterated Ministral 3B Q4_K_M), quant pipeline
(Q4_K_M+imatrix default, Q5 first remedy), LoRA still UNTRIGGERED, the five gates,
the SYSTEM precedence clause, the 4096 budget table (values ≤1000 num_keep, index
≤600, one bank ≤400 on demand, headroom ~2100–2600). Append-only growth is absorbed
by archive-move on cap/expiry — the hot files stay within budget because cold
