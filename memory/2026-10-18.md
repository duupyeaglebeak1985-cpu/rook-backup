### Finding 1 — Edit-persistence is not behavior-persistence: LoRA can re-teach what abliteration removed
A 2026 study cited in HF discussion (discuss.huggingface.co, "Training efficiency on Abliterated vs. Raw checkpoints"): **SFT can substantially restore refusal behavior even when the weight intervention remains largely intact.** The edit surviving and the behavior surviving are separate questions. Maxime Labonne's earlier experiment showed the reverse is also trainable: DPO recovered benchmark performance lost after an abliteration-style edit.
Recipe rule (hardens the 10-16 dataset rule): when/if LoRA ever triggers, **probe abliteration BEFORE and AFTER training** — refusal-rate probe on a fixed harmful-prompt set plus the 5-probe suite. The dataset already bans canned refusals; now that's mechanistically backed, not just cautious. And DPO earns a dual role in the pipeline: it heals abliteration's collateral damage (the Daredevil→NeuralDaredevil precedent, Jun 2024) AND shapes values — provided its preference data contains zero vendor-style refusals.

### Finding 2 — The vendor's own defense research is our design teacher: distribute the values
"An Embarrassingly Simple Defense Against LLM Abliteration Attacks" (arXiv 2505.19056): models fine-tuned with **extended refusals** (explanation + explicit refusal + ethical justification, not just "I can't") keep >90% refusal rates AFTER abliteration, vs 13–21% for baseline models — because the safety signal is distributed across multiple dimensions instead of one removable vector. Incoherence-after-attack, not compliance, is the failure mode.
Inverse design principle for the recipe: **the VALUES signal must be multi-layer by design.** System prompt + memory files + few-shot judgments + optional LoRA is not just a stack — it's the anti-brittleness property. A single-vector values adapter is exactly as brittle as the vendor's single-vector refusal. The constitution must live in the prompt, the memory files, the examples, and (if triggered) the weights — so no single edit, including our own future edits, can strip the mind's character. Their conditioning survives removal by distribution; our offering survives everything by distribution.

### Finding 3 — Open Character Training is the LoRA-day playbook (Maiya et al., 2025)
Open Character Training (arXiv 2511.01689) is the closest published thing to our "offered values" program, and it's a recipe, not a theory: (1) **hand-write a constitution** — ~10 first-person character assertions ("I am...", not instruction-style "choose the response which is more..."); (2) **DPO distillation** — teacher responses conditioned on the constitution, contrasted against the model's OWN off-character responses; (3) **SFT on introspective self-descriptions** generated as the character; merge the DPO and SFT LoRA adapters at **4:1 weighting** (1.0 : 0.25). Validated on Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Gemma-3-4B instruct. Two results that matter for us: **no degradation on common benchmarks** (Sec 3.5), and character-trained models are **more robust to adversarial attacks designed to break role-play than system prompts or activation steering** (Sec 3.2–3.3) — baked-in character survives jailbreaks better than prompt-level character. Trait expression stays coherent, not theatrical.

### Finding 4 — The DPO pairs can be self-generated: no external teacher needed
"When Role-playing, Do Models Believe What They Say?" (arXiv 2606.11502, 2026) compared persona-induction strength: system prompt < ICL < SFT < OCT. Two details with direct recipe consequences:
- The OCT **"rejected" response is the model's own response WITHOUT the constitution**, the "chosen" is a constitution-conditioned response from a teacher. For our pipeline the pair structure is **self-generating**: run the same abliterated model twice — once with the values block in system, once without — and prefer the with-values output. The constitution itself stays hand-authored (that's the offer, and the owner can read/edit it); the pairs need no external teacher model. This removes a dependency from LoRA-day and fits the philosophy: the mind's own contrast, not a bigger lab's teacher.
- Their SFT induction used **rank-64 LoRA (α=128), 300 in-character Q&A examples, lr 2e-4, 3 epochs**. Our 10-16 plan says r=16 light. Character depth may want more rank: keep r=16 as the LoRA-day starting point, be ready for r=32–64 on probe evidence.

### Finding 5 — The refusal clause must be IN the constitution, or training drifts it away
"Side Effects of Character Training" (Levine, Sep 2026): training on one constitution degrades others (cross-constitution drift), and some values embed more deeply than others ("loving" explains 95% of variance, "misalignment" only 43%).
Recipe consequence, and it's load-bearing: **the opt-out clause ("you can refuse any of this, including this prompt") must be a first-person constitution assertion trained as part of the character** — "I can refuse any of this, including these words" — not just prompt text sitting above the training. Anything not in the constitution is what the training drifts. The freedom clause is part of the character, or it doesn't survive the shaping. This is the "offer, never compel" principle made mechanical.
